What we coordinate

Imaging machines are at the hospital, not in our clinic. Our value is the coordination.

Digital X-ray

Suspected fractures, possible pneumonia, foreign bodies, post-fall evaluation. Often same-day.

CT scan

Detailed imaging when an X-ray is not enough: head CT after a fall, abdominal CT for severe pain.

MRI

Soft-tissue imaging for joint and spine injuries, neurological symptoms and complex orthopedic problems.

Ultrasound

Abdominal pain, gallbladder, kidneys, DVT workup, obstetric concerns and soft-tissue masses.

Imaging studies are performed by an independent licensed hospital and its radiologists. Our clinic coordinates the referral, transport and bilingual interpretation of results; we do not perform imaging on-site. For a life-threatening emergency call 911 first.

How it works

1

Bilingual assessment first

You see an English-speaking doctor at our clinic, your hotel or by video to decide what imaging you actually need.

2

We book the slot and transport

We coordinate with the hospital imaging department and arrange a ground ambulance or transfer if needed.

3

Read and explain in English

The hospital radiologist reads the study; our doctor reviews it with you and explains the next step.

Common reasons travelers need imaging

  • Possible fractures from falls, ATV accidents, scooter spills or sports injuries.
  • Head injuries with loss of consciousness, persistent headache, vomiting or confusion.
  • Severe abdominal pain.
  • Chest pain or shortness of breath.
  • Back and neck injuries.
  • Suspected DVT after a long flight.
  • Obstetric concerns for pregnant travelers.

Pricing and insurance

Imaging fees are set and billed by the hospital and vary by study. X-ray is the most affordable and MRI the most expensive. For non-emergency studies we help you get a quote in advance. Our clinic visit is quoted separately in USD.

Most international travel-insurance policies cover medically necessary imaging. You receive an itemized English invoice, and the final reimbursement decision is made by your insurer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I get an X-ray today in Cabo San Lucas?

In most urgent cases, yes. After our doctor sees you and confirms an X-ray is indicated, we fast-track a same-day appointment at the partner hospital. Walk-in self-scheduling at a hospital imaging desk in a foreign country is usually slower and confusing — calling us first is faster.

Where exactly is the imaging performed?

Imaging is performed at a leading private hospital in Los Cabos with on-site radiology — digital X-ray, CT, MRI, and ultrasound — read by qualified radiologists. We arrange the appointment, the transport if needed, and the follow-up. Our doctor reviews the results with you in English.

How much will it cost?

Imaging fees are set and billed by the hospital. X-ray is the most affordable; MRI is the most expensive; CT and ultrasound fall in between. For non-emergency studies we help you get a quote in advance. Our clinic’s coordination fee (the doctor’s visit that orders and interprets the imaging) is separate and quoted up front in USD.

Will my travel insurance pay for the imaging?

Most international travel-insurance policies cover medically necessary imaging. You will receive a fully itemized invoice in English for reimbursement. For larger studies (CT, MRI) we can help you verify coverage with our partner hospital before you commit. We coordinate the paperwork; the final reimbursement decision is made by your insurer.

Can I bring my imaging files home?

Yes — you’ll be given the imaging on a CD/USB and/or via electronic transfer, plus the radiologist’s report. We’re happy to send these to your physician at home in English on request, with your written permission.

What if the imaging shows something serious?

If the study shows something that needs hospital admission, surgery, or a specialist — we coordinate it. The same partner hospital has 24/7 emergency care, an ICU, operating rooms and a deep specialist roster. If you would prefer to fly home for treatment, we coordinate ambulance and air ambulance, including medical repatriation.
