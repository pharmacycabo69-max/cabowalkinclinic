What actually causes a hangover — and why IV helps

A hangover isn’t just “too much fun”; it’s a real physiological state. Alcohol is a diuretic, so a big night leaves you genuinely dehydrated and short on electrolytes, which drives the headache, dizziness and fatigue. Its breakdown products irritate the stomach and inflame the body, producing the nausea and that wrung-out feeling. Drinking water helps, but your gut absorbs it slowly — and slowly is the last thing you want when you’ve got a catamaran cruise or a tee time in two hours. An IV restores fluids and electrolytes directly into the bloodstream, so rehydration starts right away, while B-complex vitamins support the metabolic cleanup and anti-nausea medication settles your stomach so you can actually keep water and food down. That’s why most people feel a real shift within the 30-to-60-minute session rather than over the course of a lost morning. It’s a popular reset for bachelor and bachelorette groups especially, and because we come to your room, nobody has to face daylight or a taxi to feel better. Hydrate sensibly between drinks too — but when the morning gets away from you, this is the fastest way back.
